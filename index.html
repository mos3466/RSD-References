01 / Project Overview

RSD is a functional prototype designed to assist metro supervisors in identifying potential passenger violations, such as eating, drinking, and sitting on the floor. The system combines video-based candidate detection with trip-linked records and human verification. Its purpose is decision support, not autonomous enforcement or automatic penalties.

Problem

Continuous CCTV monitoring can require sustained manual attention; isolated visual alerts may lack a clear connection to a passenger's active trip.

Proposed Approach

Provide a smarter, safer metro environment through an integrated monitoring and trip-management solution that helps identify potential passenger violations early, supports fair human oversight, and improves the consistency of operational decisions without relying on facial recognition.

Core Contribution

Integrate trip-based evidence tracking, AI-assisted monitoring, and human-reviewed exit decisions without facial recognition.

03 / AI Models and Detection Logic

COCO-SSD

Object detection used to locate people and supported object categories in camera frames. It does not directly recognize the act of drinking or eating.

MoveNet

Human pose estimation used to derive body keypoints that can help evaluate spatial relationships and postures.

Temporal / Spatial Rules

Heuristic rules combine detected objects, pose cues, proximity, and observations across frames to flag candidate incidents for review.

Monitored Behaviors

  • Drinking: Candidate signals include a cup or bottle associated with a person and near relevant hand/face regions, with temporal context where available.
  • Eating: Candidate signals combine relevant food/object evidence with hand/face proximity and available temporal cues.
  • Floor sitting: Candidate signals use pose and posture-related evidence to identify possible sitting on the floor.
Interpretation: These are AI-assisted candidate detections, not independently validated action-classification probabilities. A displayed confidence value should not be interpreted as a calibrated probability of an actual rule violation without further validation.

05 / Trip and Evidence Workflow

  1. Entry: An RFID scan creates or identifies an active trip record with a unique Trip ID.
  2. Entry evidence: C02 may capture a time-windowed snapshot after trip creation. Missing or late evidence must not be fabricated.
  3. Detection: C01 produces potential-violation events, including camera, time, type, and available confidence information.
  4. Association: Evidence is linked only when trip and temporal conditions support the association; uncertain cases should remain unlinked or flagged for review.
  5. Review: A supervisor confirms or rejects the case.
  6. Exit: A subsequent RFID scan checks the same trip for confirmed incidents. No confirmed incident permits exit guidance; a confirmed incident results in staff referral / hold guidance.

The RFID logic includes duplicate-event handling and accidental-rescan protection. Exact cooldown and matching thresholds are implementation settings and should be verified against the final deployed code before publication as fixed values.

06 / Human Verification and Case Management

Potential violations enter the dashboard for review. Supervisors can mark a case Confirmed or Rejected. Only confirmed incidents associated with the relevant trip affect the exit decision. The system does not automatically issue fines.

Pending

Alert awaiting supervisor verification.

Confirmed

Reviewed and accepted by a supervisor; may influence trip exit guidance.

Rejected

Reviewed and dismissed; should not trigger an incident-based exit hold.

07 / Results & Evaluation

The prototype dashboard recorded 122 detection alerts. Of these, 108 were confirmed and 14 were rejected during supervisor review. The project reports an 88.5% confirmation rate for these recorded alerts.

Reported result — 88.5%: This is the dashboard-based confirmation percentage (108 confirmed alerts ÷ 122 reviewed alerts). The project team refers to this as its reported accuracy figure. Its calculation describes the reviewed alerts; independently validated model accuracy, recall and F1 require additional ground-truth evaluation, including missed violations.

Demonstrated Prototype Functions

  • RFID card scan and Trip ID creation.
  • Camera-based candidate detection and dashboard case review.
  • Trip-linked evidence handling under matching conditions.
  • LCD display of trip and staff-referral guidance.

These are functional prototype observations, not evidence of real-world metro deployment, measured response-time improvement, or validated detection accuracy.

08 / Privacy & Future Development

Privacy and Decision Support

The prototype is designed around trip identifiers rather than facial recognition. Human review is retained before confirmed incidents affect exit guidance. This design choice does not by itself establish compliance with all applicable privacy requirements.

Current Limitations

  • Heuristic action signals may produce false positives and miss real actions.
  • Camera viewpoint, lighting, occlusion, and passenger crowding can affect detection.
  • Temporal matching does not guarantee that a detected person corresponds to the RFID card holder.
  • Reported results reflect a controlled prototype, not a field trial in an operating metro system.
  • The 88.5% dashboard figure is based on reviewed alerts; a separate ground-truth benchmark is needed to quantify missed detections and independently evaluate precision, recall, and F1.

Future Integration — Bank Cards

The current physical prototype uses Darb metro cards for RFID-based trip demonstrations. A future development goal is to support compatible bank-card-based metro access and payment workflows, subject to authorized payment-system integration, security requirements, and operator approval. This functionality has not been implemented in the current prototype.

Next Evaluation Steps

  • Collect labeled positive and negative scenarios for each violation category.
  • Compute per-category confusion matrices and Precision, Recall, and F1.
  • Test multiple viewpoints, crowded scenes, and camera synchronization.
  • Evaluate privacy safeguards and operational authorization before any real deployment.
